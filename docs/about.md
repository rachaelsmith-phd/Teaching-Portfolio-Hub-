# About

## Professional Biography

I am a criminologist, instructional designer, and Ph.D. candidate in Curriculum & Instruction whose professional experience spans higher education, criminal justice, legal studies, child welfare, public service, and adult education.

For more than two decades, I have worked across colleges and universities, state agencies, courts, and community organizations. My career has included teaching criminal justice and legal studies, designing curriculum and workforce training programs, supporting child advocacy initiatives, and developing instructional systems for adult learners in both higher education and public service environments.

My work is grounded in the belief that education should prepare learners not only to understand existing systems, but also to critically evaluate, improve, and lead those systems responsibly.

# Current Scholarly Projects

## Unified Criminal Justice Curriculum Framework

An interdisciplinary curriculum model integrating justice, ethics, technology, environmental responsibility, human agency, civic responsibility, and professional identity development.

## Specialized Child Advocacy for Immigration-Involved Youth

A research and policy initiative examining best-interests advocacy models for immigration-involved children and youth.

## Children of the Sonoran Desert

A proposed child advocacy pilot integrating child welfare, educational advocacy, trauma-informed practice, and community partnerships.

## Catalytic Nodes and Group Ecology

A conceptual framework exploring emotional contagion, organizational climate, symbolic interactionism, systems thinking, and social ecology.

## Mapping the Terrain of Criminal Justice Educator Identity Transformation

Dissertation research examining how criminal justice practitioners negotiate, reconstruct, and enact educator identities following entry into higher education.

# Professional Philosophy

I view teaching, curriculum development, instructional design, and scholarship as interconnected forms of public service.

Whether working with undergraduate students, graduate students, adult learners, volunteers, faculty, or professionals, my goal is to create meaningful educational experiences that promote critical inquiry, ethical decision-making, civic responsibility, and lifelong learning.

I believe education remains one of the most powerful tools available for preparing reflective professionals capable of addressing complex challenges within their communities, organizations, and professions.
