# Teaching Effectiveness

> **Work in Progress**

## Overview

My approach to teaching effectiveness extends beyond traditional measures of student satisfaction and course completion. Effectiveness is reflected in the design of learning experiences, student engagement, authentic application of content, continuous course improvement, accessibility, and the development of professional identity.

As both an educator and instructional designer, I view teaching effectiveness as an iterative process informed by evidence, reflection, learner feedback, and continuous improvement.

# Signature Learning Experiences

## Canvas Branching Assignments

Interactive modules that allow students to navigate multiple learning pathways based on decisions and responses.

Examples may include:

- Criminal justice decision-making scenarios
- Ethical dilemma simulations
- Policy analysis activities
- Investigative pathways

### Evidence

- Student engagement
- Increased content interaction
- Multiple perspectives on complex problems

---

## Criminological Theory Simulations

Interactive "choose your own pathway" experiences designed within Canvas.

Students examine a single case using multiple theoretical perspectives including:

- Classical Theory
- Strain Theory
- Social Learning Theory
- Control Theory
- Labeling Theory
- Routine Activities Theory

### Learning Outcomes

Students learn that theory is not simply memorized content but a framework for understanding behavior, crime, and social systems.

---

## Legal Case File Projects

Drawing upon professional experience as a paralegal and legal assistant, students develop structured case files using authentic documentation practices.

Projects may include:

- Evidence organization
- Case chronology construction
- Witness analysis
- Legal research
- Professional documentation

### Learning Outcomes

- Attention to detail
- Legal analysis
- Professional communication
- Evidence-based reasoning

---

## Professional Identity Reflection

Assignments designed to encourage students to examine:

- Professional values
- Ethical responsibilities
- Leadership development
- Career trajectories
- Personal and professional identity

### Connection to Research

These projects align with my dissertation research examining criminal justice educator identity transformation and professional identity development.

# Instructional Design Effectiveness

## SAM-Based Design Process

Course development follows an iterative process informed by the Successive Approximation Model (SAM).

### Foundation Phase

- Course architecture
- Outcomes alignment
- Navigation design
- Accessibility planning

### Prototype A

- Initial build
- Content testing
- Workflow review
- Early stakeholder feedback

### Prototype B

- Revision cycle
- Expanded content
- Improved interactivity
- Accessibility refinement

### Gold Build

- Quality assurance
- Accessibility verification
- Final review
- Launch readiness

# Accessibility and Inclusive Teaching

## Accessibility as Design

Accessibility is integrated into course development rather than added at the end of the design process.

Practices include:

- Ally accessibility review
- Alternative formats
- Accessible document design
- Captioning and media review
- Universal Design for Learning principles

---

## Accessibility Improvements Through Ally

Examples include:

- Improving document accessibility
- Reducing barriers to participation
- Reviewing course materials for compliance
- Providing alternative learning pathways

### Goal

Creating learning environments where all students can meaningfully engage with course content.

# Reflective Teaching Practice

Teaching effectiveness requires continuous reflection.

Questions guiding course improvement include:

- What supported student learning?
- Where did students encounter barriers?
- Which assignments produced the richest engagement?
- How can accessibility be improved?
- How might technology better support learning?
- What revisions should occur during the next design cycle?
