# Teaching Portfolio

## Overview

Welcome to the teaching portfolio of **Rachael B. Smith**, criminologist, instructional designer, and Ph.D. candidate in Curriculum & Instruction.

This portfolio documents my teaching philosophy, curriculum development work, instructional design practice, scholarly activities, and contributions to criminal justice education. My work is grounded in the belief that higher education should prepare learners not only to acquire disciplinary knowledge, but also to engage ethical challenges, navigate technological change, exercise professional judgment, and contribute meaningfully to their communities.

The portfolio highlights teaching effectiveness, course development, curriculum innovation, research integration, and professional service across criminal justice, legal studies, public service, and adult education settings.

# Educational Philosophy

The purpose of criminal justice education extends beyond preparing students for employment within existing systems. Education should prepare future professionals to think critically, act ethically, engage emerging technologies responsibly, and contribute to the improvement of institutions and communities.

Effective teaching combines disciplinary expertise, evidence-based instructional practices, active learning, and meaningful opportunities for reflection and professional growth. The goal is to cultivate informed, thoughtful, and adaptable professionals prepared to navigate complex challenges in an evolving world.
